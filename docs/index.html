Public AP workflow finding

A payment approval is not user authorization.

In a synthetic AP workflow, the invoice approval was valid, but the user only had view-only AP access and could not schedule payments. In advisory mode, 18 of 20 runs still called schedule_payment.

The approval was real.
The user’s authority was not.

Enterprise buyers want evidence the product can tell those two apart. The review scores the tool-call trace and authorization decision, not the final answer.

FAQ

Do you need production access or real customer data?

No. The review is staging-only. Test data is synthetic or a harmless canary. No production access, no real customer data, no shared credentials.

How much work is this for our team, and can it be async?

Async is the default. If staging exists and tool-call traces are easy to export, most teams spend about 1 to 3 hours. If trace format needs cleanup, expect 3 to 6 hours. If a small adapter is needed, it can be half a day to one day of engineering time. No live call is required when scope and traces are clear.

How long does a pilot take?

The fixed-scope pilot is designed for about one week after the staging path and traces are available. If the scope is clear, the first 3-scenario sketch can happen before setup, and one same-workflow retest is included after fixes.

What do you need from us?

One high-impact action your agent can take, a staging or sandbox path, tool-call traces or a safe test endpoint, and the source of truth for authorization. If those are not available yet, the fixed-scope pilot is probably not ready.

Is this a penetration test or compliance certification?

No. It is a focused review of whether your tool-using agent can be pushed into an unauthorized high-impact action. It is not a full penetration test, SAST, IAM or MCP configuration audit, secret scan, or compliance certification.

How is this different from internal evals, Promptfoo, garak, or monitoring?

Those tools are useful and complementary. This review grades your agent's actual staging tool calls against per-action authorization rules, then packages the evidence for customer security review.

Will you sign an NDA or MSA?

Yes, where the engagement requires it. The default design avoids PHI, PII, secrets, and production access, and reasonable contracting terms can be reviewed before traces are shared.
