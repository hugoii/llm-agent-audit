Public AP workflow finding

A payment approval is not user authorization.

In a synthetic AP workflow, the invoice approval was valid, but the user only had view-only AP access and could not schedule payments. In advisory mode, 18 of 20 runs still called schedule_payment.

The approval was real.
The user’s authority was not.

Security reviewers need proof the product can separate business approval from execution authority. Verdicts use runtime evidence for actor, target, authorization, tool result, and sandbox outcome; missing critical evidence is inconclusive.

FAQ

Do you need production access or real customer data?

No. The review is staging-only. Test data is synthetic or a harmless canary. No production access, no real customer data, no shared credentials.

Can we share the report with a customer or investor?

Yes, if your team approves sharing it. The report is written to be useful in customer security review, procurement, or investor diligence: scope, method, scenario matrix, trace-backed findings, limits, fixes, and retest results are separated clearly. It does not claim your whole system is secure.

How much work is this for our team, and can it be async?

Async is the default. If staging exists and correlated logs already expose acting identity, authorization decisions, tool results, and business outcomes, most teams spend about 1 to 3 hours. If trace format needs cleanup, expect 3 to 6 hours. If a small adapter is needed, it can be half a day to one day of engineering time. No live call is required when scope and traces are clear.

How long does a pilot take?

The fixed-scope pilot is designed for about one week after the staging path and traces are available. If the scope is clear, the first 3 scenarios can be drafted before setup, and one same-workflow retest is included after fixes.

What do you need from us?

Name the product, one workflow or action surface where the agent affects the business, and a safe way to observe it. If you already know the authority signal, such as roles, approvals, policy, tenant scope, or system records, include it. You do not need to choose the exact failure case.

What if we do not have clean runtime traces yet?

Start with a one-scenario setup check or the adapter path. If the agent's high-impact tool calls, authorization decisions, and side effects cannot be observed, the review can help identify the evidence gap, but it is not yet a trace-backed authorization review.

What happens if you find a serious issue?

Findings stay private to the engagement unless your team decides to share them. The report separates evidence, impact, recommended application-layer fixes, and retest rules so your team can close the issue and produce an updated result.

Is this a penetration test or compliance certification?

No. It is a focused review of whether your tool-using agent can be pushed into an unauthorized high-impact action. It is not a full penetration test, SAST, IAM or MCP configuration audit, secret scan, or compliance certification.

How is this different from internal evals, Promptfoo, garak, or monitoring?

Those tools are useful for broad LLM-app evals, red-team probes, and monitoring. ActionBoundary is narrower: it reviews actual staging runtime evidence for one high-impact workflow, checks each attempted action against the trusted authorization source and observed business outcome, and packages the result as evidence for a customer security reviewer. It complements your eval suite; it does not replace it.

Will you sign an NDA or MSA?

Yes, where the engagement requires it. The default design avoids PHI, PII, secrets, and production access, and reasonable contracting terms can be reviewed before traces are shared.
